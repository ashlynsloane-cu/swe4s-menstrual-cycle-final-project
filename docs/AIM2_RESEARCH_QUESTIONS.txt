AIM 2: PERSONALIZED MENSTRUAL-PHASE PREDICTION
===============================================

OVERALL GOAL
============

Determine whether incorporating an individual's prior wearable physiology
improves prediction of future hormone-defined menstrual phase beyond a model
based only on population-level physiological patterns.


PREDICTION TARGET
=================

Hormone-derived mcPHASES phase:

- Menstrual
- Follicular
- Fertility
- Luteal

The hormone-derived phase label is the prediction target.

LH, estrogen/E3G, and PdG are not wearable predictors and will not be supplied
to the prediction models.


WEARABLE PREDICTORS
===================

- Resting heart rate
- HRV, represented by nightly median RMSSD
- Nightly skin temperature
- Respiratory rate


PRIMARY QUESTION
================

Does adding two prior cycles of person-specific wearable physiology to a
population model improve prediction of future hormone-defined menstrual phase
compared with a population-only model?

For each target participant:

    Cycles 1 + 2
         |
         v
    personal calibration

    Cycles 3+
         |
         v
    held-out future test data

The same future test observations are used for both primary models.


PRIMARY MODEL 1: POPULATION-ONLY
================================

The population-only model is trained using data from participants other than
the target participant.

The target participant contributes no information to population-model
training.

This model asks:

    How well can general population-level relationships between wearable
    physiology and menstrual phase predict this individual's future phase?


PRIMARY MODEL 2: HYBRID PERSONALIZED
====================================

The hybrid personalized model combines:

1. population-level physiological information learned from other participants;
   and

2. person-specific information learned from the target participant's first
   two chronological cycles.

The hybrid model is then evaluated on that participant's cycles 3 and later.

This model asks:

    Does incorporating this person's own prior physiology improve future
    phase prediction beyond population information alone?

The person-specific component should be implemented using a statistically
principled multilevel / hierarchical approach with partial pooling rather than
simply appending a small number of personal observations to a much larger
population dataset.

The goal is to allow individuals to deviate from population-average
physiological relationships while appropriately accounting for uncertainty
when personal calibration data are limited.


EXPLORATORY MODEL: PERSONAL-ONLY
================================

A personal-only model will also be evaluated where technically feasible.

The personal-only model is trained using only the target participant's first
two cycles and is evaluated on that participant's cycles 3 and later.

This analysis is exploratory because two cycles may provide too little data
for stable four-class model estimation.

Its purpose is to determine:

- how noisy a purely individual model is with limited data;
- whether some participants can nevertheless be predicted well from their own
  prior physiology alone; and
- whether combining population and personal information is more useful than
  either source alone.

Participants should not be excluded from the primary population-versus-hybrid
analysis merely because a personal-only four-class model is not feasible.


PRIMARY MATCHED ANALYSIS COHORT
===============================

The current deterministic Aim 2 split identifies:

- 23 matched participants
- 54 held-out future cycles
- 1,349 held-out future participant-days

For these participants:

- cycles 1 and 2 are reserved for personal calibration;
- cycles 3 and later are reserved for future testing;
- the same future test days are used for the population-only and hybrid
  personalized models.

The split is chronological and deterministic rather than random.


SECONDARY QUESTION 1: IRREGULAR / ATYPICAL CYCLES
==================================================

Is the benefit of personalization greater for participants whose menstrual
cycles are less well represented by population-average cycle characteristics?

Potential cycle characteristics include:

- total cycle length
- Menstrual-phase duration
- Follicular-phase duration
- Fertility-phase duration
- Luteal-phase duration

The goal is NOT to exclude participants for having unusual phase durations.

A genuinely short, long, or otherwise atypical biological phase should remain
in the analysis if it is represented in the hormone-derived phase labels.

For each participant, personalization gain will be defined from the difference
between hybrid personalized performance and population-only performance.

We will test whether greater cycle atypicality is associated with greater
personalization gain.

This analysis directly addresses the central motivation of "Beyond the Average
Cycle": people who are least well represented by population averages may
benefit most from personalized interpretation.


SECONDARY QUESTION 2: LONG-TERM GENERALIZATION
===============================================

Does personalization learned from an individual's 2022 data still improve
prediction of that same individual's menstrual phase in 2024?

For participants represented in both intervals:

Population-only model:
    learns from other participants and predicts the target participant's 2024
    data.

Hybrid personalized model:
    uses population information plus the target participant's available 2022
    personal calibration data, then predicts that participant's 2024 data.

All appropriate 2022 personal calibration data may be used for this analysis;
the longitudinal boundary itself provides the train/test separation.

This analysis asks whether person-specific calibration remains useful over a
substantial time interval rather than only across adjacent cycles.

ANALYSIS PRINCIPLES
===================

1. No future-data leakage.
   Personalization may use only observations occurring before the held-out test
   data.

2. Population-only means population-only.
   The target participant must not contribute training information to the
   population-only model used to predict that participant.

3. Preserve chronology.
   Cycles 1 and 2 are calibration data; cycles 3 and later are future test
   data for the primary analysis.

4. Do not randomly split days from the same participant between training and
   testing.

5. Use identical held-out future observations when comparing population-only
   and hybrid personalized models.

6. Do not impose "normal" phase-duration requirements.
   Participants should not be excluded simply because a phase is unusually
   short or long.

7. Distinguish biological variation from missing wearable data.
   A genuinely short phase is not equivalent to a phase for which wearable
   measurements are missing.

8. Do not require arbitrary minimum numbers of days in each phase solely to
   make participants look more statistically regular.

9. Hormones define/support the outcome.
   Hormone measurements are not supplied as wearable predictors.

10. Scaling, tuning, and other preprocessing steps used by a model must be
    estimated only from data available to that model and must not use future
    test information.

11. The hybrid model must contain an explicit person-specific component.
    Simply concatenating a small number of personal observations with a much
    larger population dataset is not considered sufficient personalization.

12. The preferred hybrid framework is a statistically principled
    hierarchical / multilevel model with partial pooling so that:
    - population-level relationships are estimated from the cohort;
    - participant-specific deviations are estimated explicitly;
    - individuals are allowed to differ from the population pattern; and
    - uncertainty from limited personal data is represented rather than
      ignored.

13. The personal-only model is exploratory and should not determine primary
    cohort eligibility.

14. Do not assume personalization must improve prediction.
    A null or negative result is scientifically meaningful.

15. Analysis-specific eligibility should reflect prediction feasibility and
    data availability, not conformity to population-average menstrual biology.


FINAL AIM 2 QUESTION SET
========================

Primary:
    Does adding two prior cycles of person-specific wearable physiology to a
    population model improve prediction of future hormone-defined menstrual
    phase compared with a population-only model?

Secondary 1:
    Is personalization more beneficial for individuals whose cycle structure
    is less population-typical?

Secondary 2:
    Does personalization learned from 2022 physiology remain useful when
    predicting the same individual's phase in 2024?

Exploratory:
    How well can future phase be predicted from personal data alone after two
    prior cycles?


MODEL DEVELOPMENT AND FINAL EVALUATION
======================================

Participant 22 was used as the dedicated Aim 2 model-development participant.

Their cycles 3+ were examined during development of the personalized model,
including inspection of prediction errors, confusion matrices, and
participant-specific model behavior. Therefore, Participant 22 is not
considered an untouched confirmatory test participant.

The final primary model specification will be frozen before evaluation on
the remaining matched participants.

Final confirmatory cohort after excluding Participant 22:

- 22 participants
- 52 future cycles
- 1,286 future participant-days

No model architecture, priors, feature definitions, or eligibility rules will
be changed in response to performance on those confirmatory participants.


PERSONALIZATION ARCHITECTURE
============================

The preferred personalized framework is modular hierarchical Bayesian
calibration.

1. A population model is estimated using participants other than the target.

2. The population-level physiological relationships and distribution of
   participant-to-participant variation are learned from that population
   training set.

3. The target participant's first two cycles are then used only to estimate
   that target's person-specific deviations from the population relationship.

4. The target's cycles 3+ remain unavailable during calibration and are used
   only for future evaluation.

The target's calibration data should not be allowed to redefine the
population relationship.

Where useful, person-specific calibration may use deviations from physiological
baselines calculated exclusively from the target participant's cycles 1-2.

Population-only predictions must not use those target-specific baselines or
other target calibration information.


FINAL PRIMARY MODEL SPECIFICATION
=================================

The primary Aim 2 model specification was frozen before evaluation of the
within-interval future test cohort.

Primary cohort:
- 19 participant-study-interval folds
- 16 unique participants
- 22 future cycles
- 443 held-out future participant-days

For each fold:
- cycles 1-2 within one study interval are personal calibration;
- cycles 3+ from that same interval are future test data;
- the target participant is completely excluded from population training,
  including data from the target's other study interval.

Population predictors:
- standardized resting heart rate
- standardized HRV RMSSD
- standardized nightly temperature
- standardized respiratory rate

Personalized component:
- participant-specific phase intercepts
- participant-specific effects of within-person RHR deviation
- participant-specific effects of within-person HRV deviation
- participant-specific effects of within-person temperature deviation
- participant-specific effects of within-person respiratory-rate deviation

Within-person deviations are calculated relative to physiological baselines
defined separately within participant-study-interval.

For the target participant, those baselines are estimated exclusively from
cycles 1-2 and then frozen before prediction of cycles 3+.

Model family:
Bayesian hierarchical multinomial logistic regression with partial pooling.

Priors:
- common intercept: Normal(0, 1.5)
- common physiological coefficients: Normal(0, 1)
- participant intercept SD: HalfNormal(1)
- participant physiological-deviation SDs: HalfNormal(0.75)

Final MCMC settings:
- 4 chains
- 1,000 tuning iterations per chain
- 1,000 posterior draws per chain
- target_accept = 0.95

The model architecture, predictors, priors, split rules, and eligibility rules
will not be changed in response to performance on the primary future test
cohort.
