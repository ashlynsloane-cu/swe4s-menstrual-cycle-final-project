AIM 2: PERSONALIZED MENSTRUAL-PHASE PREDICTION
===============================================

Overall goal
------------
Determine whether incorporating an individual's prior wearable physiology
improves prediction of future hormone-defined menstrual phase beyond a model
based only on population-level physiological patterns.

Prediction target
-----------------
Hormone-derived mcPHASES phase:

- Menstrual
- Follicular
- Fertility
- Luteal

Wearable predictors
-------------------
- Resting heart rate
- HRV (nightly median RMSSD)
- Nightly skin temperature
- Respiratory rate


PRIMARY QUESTION
================

Does adding limited person-specific calibration data improve prediction of
future hormone-defined menstrual phase beyond a population-only model?

Primary model comparison:

1. Population-only model
   - Learns physiological relationships from other participants.
   - The target participant is excluded from population-model training.

2. Hybrid personalized model
   - Uses population information plus the target participant's own earlier
     labeled wearable observations.
   - Represents the realistic use case in which a wearable begins with
     population knowledge and becomes personalized after approximately
     1-2 observed cycles.

Both models must be evaluated on the SAME held-out future days for the target
participant.

We will explicitly compare personalization after one prior cycle versus after
two prior cycles when the data permit.


SECONDARY QUESTION 1: IRREGULAR / ATYPICAL CYCLES
==================================================

Is the benefit of personalization greater for participants whose menstrual
cycles are less well represented by population-average cycle characteristics?

Examples of cycle characteristics that may be considered include:

- Total cycle length
- Menstrual-phase duration
- Follicular-phase duration
- Fertility-phase duration
- Luteal-phase duration

The goal is NOT to exclude participants for having unusual phase durations.

A short or otherwise atypical biological phase should remain in the analysis
if it is genuinely represented in the hormone-derived phase labels.

We will ask whether personalization gain:

    hybrid performance - population-only performance

is associated with how atypical an individual's cycle characteristics are
relative to the cohort.

This analysis directly tests the motivation behind "Beyond the Average Cycle":
people who are least well represented by population averages may benefit most
from personalized interpretation.


SECONDARY QUESTION 2: LONG-TERM GENERALIZATION
===============================================

Does personalization learned from an individual's 2022 data still improve
prediction of that same individual's menstrual phase in 2024?

For participants with data in both intervals:

- Population-only model:
  learns from other participants and predicts the target participant in 2024.

- Hybrid personalized model:
  uses population information plus the target participant's earlier 2022 data,
  then predicts that participant's 2024 data.

This tests whether person-specific calibration remains useful across a
substantial time interval rather than only across adjacent cycles.


EXPLORATORY QUESTION: PERSONAL-ONLY MODEL
=========================================

How well can future menstrual phase be predicted using only an individual's
own prior wearable physiology?

Personal-only model:
- Trained only on the target participant's earlier labeled wearable data.
- Evaluated on that participant's later unseen data.

This is NOT expected to be the most practical primary model because only
1-2 prior cycles may provide limited training data.

Its purpose is to determine:

- how noisy a purely individual model is with limited calibration data;
- whether some participants can nevertheless be predicted well from their
  own prior physiology alone; and
- whether combining personal and population information is more useful than
  either source alone.

==========================

For a target participant:

ANALYSIS PRINCIPLES
===================

1. No future-data leakage.
   Personalization may use only observations occurring before the test data.

2. Population-only means population-only.
   The target participant must not contribute training information to the
   population-only model used to predict that participant.

3. Do not impose "normal" phase-duration requirements.
   Participants should not be excluded simply because a phase is unusually
   short or long.

4. Distinguish biological variation from missing wearable data.
   A genuinely short phase is not equivalent to a phase for which wearable
   measurements are missing.

5. Hormone measurements define/support the prediction target.
   LH, estrogen/E3G, and PdG are not wearable predictors and should not be
   supplied to the prediction model.

6. Compare models on identical held-out observations whenever possible.
   Differences in performance should reflect the model, not different test
   datasets.

7. Participant chronology must be preserved.
   Randomly splitting days from the same participant into training and test
   sets would leak person-specific information and overestimate performance.

8. Do not assume personalization must help.
   A null result is scientifically meaningful.

9. Treat the personal-only model as exploratory unless the available personal
   training data prove sufficient for stable estimation.

10. Analysis-specific eligibility should reflect prediction feasibility and
    data availability, not conformity to population-average menstrual biology.
